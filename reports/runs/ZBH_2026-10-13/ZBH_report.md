## 0. Two-Minute Drill (Lynch)
**Slow Grower drifting down.** The organic growth guide is 2.25–3.25%, GAAP EPS fell about 15% a year from FY23 to FY25, and knees are flat. **Why own it:** ZBH is a scaled hip-and-knee franchise with a 70% gross margin, and it looks cheap on adjusted earnings (10.8x forward). **What has to happen:** to justify $92, margins must climb back above the FY23 peak (30.8% EBITDA against 27.2% today), or revenue must compound at about 6% for a decade, something it hasn't done organically in any 4-year window since 2016. **What could go wrong:** that is already happening. Adjusted operating margin is down 210bp, and management is buying back stock at about 1.5x our value, partly with borrowed money.

## 2. Business Quality (Buffett)
**Moat: Narrow, eroding.** Gross margin is stable at 69.7–71.8%, but adjusted operating margin fell 210bp year on year in Q2 and gross margin is down 2.1pp since FY23. **ROIC vs WACC:** reported ROIC is 4.7% (FY25) against an 8.5% WACC, below every year. Excluding goodwill (51% of invested capital), ROIC is about 9.6%, only marginally above WACC, so acquired capital hasn't earned its cost. **Circle of competence:** yes. The business is understandable and predictable enough to model, and the question is price and capital allocation.

Terminal growth is 2.0% / 3.0% / 3.5% (bear / base / bull), and terminal value is 47–56% of EV. Capex is **7.8% of revenue including instruments**. ## 5. The Skeptic's Audit
- **Number audit:** 16 checked, 3 failed, 1 load-bearing.
  - **The load-bearing failure was capex.** "Additions to instruments" ($300.5M in FY25, $322.9M TTM) is a separate investing line in the 10-K and 10-Q. It is real capex: surgical instrument sets placed with hospitals. **The orchestrator's mid-run capex "correction" also missed it.** It's a company-specific XBRL tag that the SEC data feed doesn't show. Base value went from $95.27 to $71.58 (−24.9%).
  - **The minor failures:** FY20/21 cash flows were mixed with pre-ZimVie-spin figures, and revolver status was stale (renewed to 2031, plus a new $1.25B 364-day facility).
- **Capital Allocation Grade: D.**
  - The $1 retained-earnings test fails badly: **−$5.75 of market value per $1 retained** (FY20→FY25).
  - Buybacks averaged **$105.5 a share**, about 48% above value, and the program is about −$320M marked to market.
  - FY25 payouts plus M&A were $2.1B against $1.1B of corrected FCF. Net debt rose from $5.7B to $7.1B.
  - Goodwill and intangible impairments totalled $1.86B over 2018–22. Goodwill is 43.5% of assets.
  - **Insiders** (Form 4s parsed): zero open-market buys and $1.58M of sales in 12 months. Two senior field leaders also left.
  - It isn't an F, because buybacks did shrink the share count by more than SBC added and leverage stays serviceable (3.06x against a 4.5x covenant).
- **Quality of Earnings:**
  - SBC is low: 5.6% of OCF.
  - Accruals aren't a red flag: GAAP net income is depressed by $530–666M a year of non-cash acquired-intangible amortisation.
  - **Recurring "non-recurring" charges** (restructuring plus acquisition costs) ran at 2.3% / 3.2% / 3.1% of sales in FY23–25, and they are costs. Q2 adjusted net income was about 2x GAAP.
  - **Receivables are an amber flag:** up 12.3% organically against sales up 7.2%, and DSO went from 70 to 76 days.
- **Bear thesis:** the price assumes FY23-peak-plus margins while the trend runs the other way, and capital is being returned above value with borrowed money.
- **Primary Downside Catalysts:**
  1. **Margin squeeze in hips and knees** (pricing, China volume-based procurement, knee share). Signpost: TTM GAAP EBITDA margin below **25.5%** for two straight quarters, or US knee growth below 2% for 4 quarters.
  2. **Leverage-funded payouts or another large deal.** Signpost: net debt / EBITDA above **3.5x**, an acquisition over $1B, or a second year of buybacks plus dividends exceeding FCF. FY26's plan of $1.19B against $1.08B FCF is already on track to breach.
