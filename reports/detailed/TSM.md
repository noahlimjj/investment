**Exchange:** NYSE (ADR) | **Price (16 Aug 2026):** $425.22 | **Currency:** USD

---

## PART 1: LYNCH CLASSIFICATION
**Category: Fast Grower (with characteristics of a Wide-Moat Stalwart)**

TSMC has grown revenue at **40%+ in USD terms in 2026**, with net income +77% YoY in Q2 2026. This is Fast Grower territory by any Lynch metric. However, the business has such structural dominance (90% of the world's most advanced chip production) that it is maturing into something sturdier — a structurally essential monopoly analogous to a Stalwart in moat permanence, but with Fast Grower earnings velocity. Record monthly revenue July 2026: NT$467.58B (+44.7% YoY).

**Is classification changing?** At some point the AI capex cycle will peak, and TSMC will decelerate into a high-single-digit Stalwart. We are NOT there yet. The 2nm ramp and AI accelerator demand ensure Fast Grower status persists through at least 2028.

---

## PART 2: THE MOAT (Buffett)
**Rating: WIDE — One of the widest moats in the global economy.**

- **Technological Monopoly:** Controls ~90% of the world's most advanced chip production (3nm, 2nm). Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm — all depend on TSMC with no viable alternative.
- **Market Share:** 70–72% of the global pure-play foundry market. Widening, not narrowing, despite Samsung and Intel spending billions to compete.
- **Advanced Packaging (CoWoS):** Essential for AI accelerators (H100, H200, Blackwell). No competitor can replicate this at volume.
- **Pure-play model:** TSMC does not compete with its customers, creating deep trust and co-development partnerships. Customers share roadmaps years in advance.
- **Switching Costs:** Migrating a chip design from TSMC takes 2-3 years and hundreds of millions. Customers don't switch.
- **70% of HBM4 allocation** for NVIDIA's next-gen Rubin platform is already assigned to TSMC's packaging.

**Moat trend: Widening.** Every generation of AI chip cements TSMC's position further.

## PART 3: MANAGEMENT QUALITY (Buffett)
**Rating: Exceptional**

- **Capital allocation:** $60-64B CapEx in 2026 — ROE 35%, gross margins 67.7% confirm returns justify spend. Consistent quarterly cash dividends maintained through heavy capex cycle (NT$7.0/share, Q2 2026).
- **Candor:** Monthly revenue updates, precise technology ramp timelines. Granular disclosure, no spin.
- **Institutional Imperative check:** Resisted US pressure to build in the US at a pace beyond capacity. Committed eventually, but on their own terms — rational, independent judgment.
- **Insider buying:** C-suite (CEO, CFO, COO) actively buying in 2025-2026. Broad-based, described as "resounding affirmation."
- **Founder legacy:** Morris Chang's pure-play model remains intact 40 years on. Competitors failed to replicate it.

## PART 8: BULL CASE vs BEAR CASE

**Bull Case:**
1. AI demand sustains — TSMC is the only bottleneck; pricing power drives gross margins toward 70%+
2. 2nm ASP uplift: each wafer generates 30-40% more revenue vs 3nm
3. Arizona fab completion reduces geopolitical discount; P/E re-rates from 21x to 28x = ~$640 stock
4. CoWoS advanced packaging becomes independently moat-level — a standalone franchise

**Bear Case:**
1. China military action in Taiwan Strait → existential 60-70% drawdown scenario
2. AI capex cycle peak in 2027-2028 → revenue decelerates to 10-15% growth
3. Intel Foundry or Samsung achieves 2nm-competitive yields at commercial scale within 5 years
4. US export restrictions force operational restructuring; customer relationships at risk

**Single biggest variable:** Taiwan Strait military stability. All other risks are manageable in isolation.
