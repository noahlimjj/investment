## PART 1: LYNCH CLASSIFICATION
**Category: Cyclical with Fast Grower characteristics (AI Memory Supercycle phase)**

Memory semiconductors are textbook Lynch Cyclicals — they follow boom/bust cycles tied to supply/demand imbalances. However, SK Hynix has a unique characteristic in 2026: its HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) division is NOT behaving cyclically. HBM is a near-monopoly product supplying NVIDIA, AMD, and Google with AI accelerator memory. HBM revenue is growing at 250%+ and is long-term contracted (LTAs). Lynch would note this is a Cyclical in its commodity DRAM/NAND division, but a Fast Grower in HBM.

**Classification change:** The HBM segment is structurally growing faster than the cyclical DRAM market. If HBM reaches 40%+ of revenue (currently ~30%), SK Hynix transitions classification from Cyclical to Fast Grower — permanently. This re-rating is not priced in.

---

## PART 2: THE MOAT (Buffett)
**Rating: NARROW in commodity DRAM/NAND → WIDE in HBM**

**HBM moat (Wide):**
- **57-62% market share** in HBM globally. Projected to hold **~70% of HBM4** for NVIDIA's next-gen Rubin platform.
- **TSV (Through-Silicon Via) mastery:** First to productionise and achieve high-yield TSV bonding. Samsung has tried to replicate — still 1.5-2 years behind in HBM yields.
- **"Golden Triangle" partnerships:** Co-design with NVIDIA for HBM architecture + packaging integration with TSMC = lock-in that customers cannot easily break.
- **Switching costs:** NVIDIA H200/H100 and Blackwell systems are architecture-optimised around SK Hynix HBM. Re-qualifying another supplier takes 12-18 months and risks yields.
- **Long-Term Agreements (LTAs):** HBM4 is sold via multi-year contracts. Revenue visibility is 2-3 years.

**Commodity DRAM moat (Narrow):**
- Three-player oligopoly (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron). Pricing is cyclical. No durable moat here.
- Commodity DRAM bears the full cycle risk.

## PART 7: SECTOR-SPECIFIC EDGE
- HBM4 ships in 2026; HBM4E in 2027 — each generation carries 30-40% premium pricing vs prior generation
- NVIDIA "Rubin" platform requires HBM4 — SK Hynix is the primary qualified supplier
- TSV yield rates are ~95% at SK Hynix vs ~70-75% at Samsung — this means SK Hynix produces more usable chips per wafer. This is the manufacturing moat in numbers.
- AI inference demand (post-training) is LESS memory intensive than training — watch this as a downside risk if inference chips dominate new orders

## PART 8: BULL CASE vs BEAR CASE

**Bull Case:**
1. HBM4/4E pricing stays premium through 2027-2028 as NVIDIA/Google demand exceeds production capacity → revenue sustains ₩300T+ annually
2. SK Hynix takes 70% of HBM4 market → Samsung falls further behind → pricing power amplifies
3. Analyst price target of ₩3.16M achieved over 12 months → +90% return
4. AI inference chips (cheaper, mass volume) create a NEW memory market alongside training HBM

**Bear Case:**
1. Samsung achieves HBM4 yields at scale in 2027 → supply floods → ASP collapses 30-40%
2. US export controls on Korean memory to China tighten → significant revenue loss (China was ~30% of pre-HBM revenue)
3. AI capex cycle peaks → NVIDIA orders slow → HBM demand drops
4. Memory cycle downturn in commodity DRAM drags down blended margins

**Single biggest variable:** Samsung's HBM yield improvement timeline. If Samsung reaches 90%+ HBM4 yields by Q1 2027, the pricing premium at SK Hynix compresses materially.
