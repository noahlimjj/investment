# Revolution Medicines (RVMD) — Deep Dive

**Date:** 28 August 2026 · **Price:** $221.15 (27 Aug close, all-time high) · **Market cap:** $47.4B · **EV:** ~$44.0B
**Shares out:** 214.3M (31 Jul 2026) · **Cash:** $3.94B · **52-wk range:** $35.62 – $221.15

> **This analysis is two days old in the most literal sense.** The FDA approved daraxonrasib (**RASONQUE**) on **26 August 2026** — 6.5 months ahead of its PDUFA date via the FDA Commissioner's National Priority Voucher. RVMD stopped being a clinical-stage biotech 48 hours ago. Every pre-approval valuation framework on this name is obsolete.

---

## 1. What just happened

Revolution Medicines is a RAS(ON) inhibitor company. RAS is the most frequently mutated oncogene in human cancer and was considered undruggable for forty years. RVMD's tri-complex approach — the drug binds cyclophilin A, and that binary complex then binds the *active, GTP-bound* state of RAS — is a genuine scientific breakthrough, not a me-too.

**RASolute 302** (Phase 3, 2L metastatic PDAC, n=500, 1:1 vs physician's-choice chemo):

| Endpoint | RASONQUE (n=248) | Chemo (n=252) | HR |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Median OS** | **13.2 mo** | 6.7 mo | **0.40** (0.30–0.53) |
| Median PFS (BICR) | 7.2 mo | 3.6 mo | 0.49 (0.38–0.64) |
| ORR | 30% | 11% | p<0.0001 |
| OS events | 79 (32%) | 141 (56%) | |

Doubling overall survival in second-line metastatic pancreatic cancer is, without exaggeration, the largest advance in this disease in decades. It earned an ASCO 2026 Plenary slot, simultaneous NEJM publication, and a standing ovation. Chemo comparators were real regimens (mFOLFIRINOX, gem/nab-paclitaxel, FOLFOX, nal-IRI).

**The label is broader than most coverage has reported.** From the actual US PI:

> *"...indicated for the treatment of adult patients with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who have received at least one prior systemic therapy **or** who are not candidates for multiagent systemic therapy."*

That is **"or", not "and"** — several outlets (including BioPharma Dive) described this as a "relatively narrow second-line indication." It isn't. It covers 2L+ *plus* first-line patients unfit for combination chemotherapy, a large and underserved group. There is also **no companion diagnostic and no RAS-mutation requirement** — despite the trial's primary population being RAS G12 mutant. No contraindications.

**Pricing:** $39,800 WAC per 30-day supply ≈ **$478k/year list**.

**Tolerability** — the underrated part of the story. Rash 87% (13% G3/4), diarrhea 67%, stomatitis 56%. Those look ugly, but the number that matters is **discontinuation for adverse events of just 2.9%**. Dose interruptions 69% and reductions 37% mean it needs active management, but patients stay on drug. Versus chemo it showed *less* peripheral neuropathy (10% vs 29%) and *less* fatigue (47% vs 61%). Watch items: GI perforation and ILD/pneumonitis warnings, plus one case of renal thrombotic microangiopathy.

It is a tolerable cost of non-dilutive capital, not a structural impairment.

## 3. The pipeline is the actual investment case

Daraxonrasib in 2L PDAC alone does not justify $44B of enterprise value. The thesis is that RAS(ON) inhibition becomes a franchise across the most commonly mutated oncogene in oncology.

| Asset | Target | Program | Stage | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Daraxonrasib** | RAS(ON) multi | **2L+ mPDAC** | **APPROVED 26 Aug 2026** | launched |
| Daraxonrasib | | RASolute 303 — 1L metastatic PDAC | Ph3 enrolling | — |
| Daraxonrasib | | RASolute 304 — adjuvant PDAC | Ph3 enrolling | — |
| Daraxonrasib | | RASolve 301 — 2L RAS-mut NSCLC | Ph3, enrollment completes 2026 | **readout 2027** |
| **Zoldonrasib** | G12D | RASolute 305 (1L PDAC), 309 (2L doublet), RASolve 308 (1L NSCLC) | Ph3 | — |
| **Elironrasib** | G12C | RASolve 307 — 1L NSCLC + SOC | Ph3 starts Q4 2026 | — |
| RMC-5127 | G12V | first-in-human | Ph1 | RP2D 2H26, data 2027 |
| RM-055 | novel class | IND-stage | FIH Q4 2026 | — |

**The Phase 1 NSCLC combination data is the most important underappreciated datapoint here:**

- **Elironrasib** (G12C) + pembrolizumab + platinum doublet, 1L NSCLC: **confirmed ORR 85%**, DCR 97%, **95% of patients progression-free at 6 months** (n=39, median follow-up 8.7 months).
- **Zoldonrasib** (G12D) + pembro + chemo, 1L NSCLC: **ORR 82%**, DCR 100% (n=28 evaluable, median follow-up only 3.4 months — early).

Both with "minimal added toxicity and a favorable liver safety profile" — notable because liver toxicity has been the recurring failure mode for KRAS-inhibitor/IO combinations across the industry. Responses of 82–85% in 1L NSCLC, if they hold in Phase 3, would be franchise-defining. FDA also granted daraxonrasib Breakthrough Therapy Designation in previously treated non-G12C RAS-mutant NSCLC.

Also running: CRC strategy update due Q4 2026, and collaborations with Summit (ivonescimab PD-1/VEGF bispecific), Tango (PRMT5) and BMS.

## 4. Competition

**In PDAC, near-term competition is thin.** Lilly's olomorasib got BTD in previously treated KRAS G12C pancreatic cancer, but G12C is only ~1–2% of PDAC — daraxonrasib's multi-selective profile covers G12D/G12V/G12R, which is the bulk of the disease. Immuneering (IMM-1-104) publicly repositioned toward 1L on the day of RVMD's approval. Nothing else is close to registration in 2L PDAC.

**In NSCLC, competition is fierce.** Sotorasib and adagrasib are established (though neither became a big seller), with divarasib (Roche), olomorasib (Lilly), glecirasib, garsorasib, calderasib and elisrasib all advancing. Olomorasib posted 1L NSCLC ORRs of 55.6–76.9% at ASCO 2026 — below elironrasib's 85%, but same ballpark, and Lilly has vastly more commercial muscle. RVMD's structural edge is breadth: G12C + G12D + G12V + multi-selective covers >70% of RAS-mutant NSCLC, where rivals are mostly G12C-only.

**The real long-term risk is not a better RAS drug — it's that big pharma out-executes commercially.** RVMD is building a global oncology sales force from scratch against Lilly, Roche, BMS and Amgen.

### Reverse DCF — what is priced in

> **At $221.15, the market is discounting roughly $15–18B of peak worldwide sales** ($15.3B at a 7.5% WACC, $17.3B at 9.5%, $18.3B at 10.5%).

Compare that to the sell-side: **$5.0–7.6B consensus for PDAC**, Inflection Labs at $8.5B for PDAC, and RBC's **~$11.5B all-indication peak** (RBC also models Q3-26 revenue of $28M, Q4-26 $148M, and $1.1B by end-2027). **The current price sits above even the most bullish published peak-sales estimate.**

**Cross-check on EV/peak-sales**, the standard biotech shorthand — typical range is 2–4x:

| Peak sales assumption | EV/peak sales at $44B EV |
|---|---|
| $7.6B (PDAC consensus high) | **5.8x** — expensive |
| $11.5B (RBC all-indication) | **3.8x** — roughly fair |
| $18.0B (bull franchise) | **2.4x** — cheap |

Both methods agree: today's price requires you to underwrite something at or beyond the top of the published range.

**Where the value is most sensitive:** launch ramp speed matters less than you'd think (peak in 2032 vs 2036 moves base case only $124 → $93), and discount rate matters surprisingly little. **Peak sales is the whole ballgame** — which means RASolve 301 and RASolute 303 are the entire investment case.

## 6. Bull case

1. **The 2L PDAC data is not incremental — it is categorical.** HR 0.40 for overall survival in a disease where nothing has worked. Median OS is still immature (only 32% events in the treatment arm; 95% CI 10.0–NE), so the 13.2-month figure could *improve* with follow-up.
2. **The label is broad and needs no diagnostic.** 2L+ all-comers *plus* 1L-unfit, no RAS testing, no contraindications. Every metastatic PDAC patient in the US is a potential candidate at second line without a biomarker gate — an enormous practical advantage for launch velocity.
3. **Demand is already demonstrated.** The Expanded Access Program distributed drug for **2,000+ patients within three weeks of opening**, across nearly all 50 states and both academic and community settings. That is a pre-built launch curve; RBC's $148M Q4 estimate may prove conservative.
4. **NSCLC could dwarf pancreatic.** 85% confirmed ORR with elironrasib and 82% with zoldonrasib in 1L NSCLC combinations, with clean liver safety, against a market several times the size of PDAC. RASolve 301 reads out in 2027.
5. **Four shots on goal, one mechanism.** Dara (multi), eliro (G12C), zoldo (G12D), RMC-5127 (G12V), plus RM-055. RAS is mutated in ~25% of all human cancers. This is a platform, not a product.
6. **Balance sheet is de-risked.** $3.9B cash + $1.5B committed, revenue starting now. No financing overhang for years.
7. **Takeout optionality.** A first-in-class approved RAS franchise is exactly what large-cap pharma facing 2028–2030 patent cliffs needs to buy.

**Bull value: $267 (+21%).**

## 7. Bear case

1. **The stock has already priced the bull case.** Up 165% YTD, roughly 6x off the 52-week low of $35.62, at an all-time high, on the day of approval. The reverse DCF requires $15–18B peak sales — above every published estimate. There is no margin of safety; you are buying the outcome, not the option on it.
2. **2L PDAC alone is a modest commercial opportunity.** US metastatic PDAC 2L-eligible is roughly 14,000 patients/year (≈68k US cases, ~50% metastatic at diagnosis, ~70% treated in 1L, ~45% fit enough for 2L). Even at a $478k list price with 60–65% penetration, US 2L is a ~$2–2.5B business. The $44B EV needs 1L, adjuvant and lung.
3. **The 1L-unfit population is a label extrapolation.** RASolute 302 required **ECOG 0–1 and one prior line**. Patients "not candidates for multiagent systemic therapy" are, by definition, frailer than anyone studied. Real-world tolerability in ECOG 2 patients — against a drug causing 87% rash, 67% diarrhea, 69% dose interruptions — is genuinely unknown. Uptake there may disappoint.
4. **Tolerability requires active management.** 69% dose interruptions and 37% dose reductions are fine in trial centers with dedicated staff. Community oncology, where most PDAC is treated, is a different environment. Persistence may be worse than the 2.9% discontinuation rate implies.
5. **1L PDAC is a much harder trial.** RASolute 303 pits daraxonrasib against mFOLFIRINOX/gem-nab-paclitaxel in fit patients where chemo genuinely works. A 2L win against a weak comparator does not guarantee a 1L win against a strong one. Base case assumes only partial success here; failure takes you toward the bear.
6. **NSCLC is crowded and the data is early.** Zoldonrasib's 82% ORR came from 28 evaluable patients at 3.4 months median follow-up. Single-arm Phase 1 ORRs in IO combinations routinely fail to translate into Phase 3 PFS/OS wins — much of that 82% is pembro+chemo doing the work. Lilly, Roche, BMS and Amgen are all in this market with more commercial firepower.
7. **Execution risk on a from-scratch global launch.** Building oncology commercial infrastructure across the US, EU and Japan simultaneously, while running eight-plus Phase 3 trials, on a ~$2.2B opex base that must keep growing.
8. **Downside is severe and asymmetric.** If 1L PDAC and NSCLC both disappoint, the disciplined bear (R&D cut to ~$900M–1.1B) is worth $29–45/share. The bear case is not a 20% drawdown — it is −80% or worse.

**Thesis flipper:** RASolve 301 (2L NSCLC) missing in 2027, or RASolute 303 (1L PDAC) failing. Either would collapse the peak-sales assumption from ~$11B toward ~$5B and take fair value to roughly $50.

## 8. Verdict

### HOLD if owned / DO NOT INITIATE at $221 — trim into strength

| | |
|---|---|
| **Base-case fair value** | **$108** (−51%) |
| Probability-weighted (25/45/30) | $132 (−40%) |
| Bull / Bear | $267 / $14–45 |
| Priced-in peak sales | **~$15–18B** vs $11.5B most-bullish published |
| EV / peak sales | 5.8x on consensus PDAC; 3.8x on RBC all-in |
| Sell-side mean target | ~$223 (Strong Buy, 21 buy / 0 sell) |
| **Accumulate below** | **$120** |
| **Strong buy below** | **$80** |
| **Trim above** | **$230** |

**Reasoning.** The science here is real and the approval is a genuine landmark — doubling survival in second-line metastatic pancreatic cancer, with a broad diagnostic-free label, is the most important oncology approval of 2026. I do not doubt the drug. I doubt the price.

At $221 the market has already capitalised the full franchise: 1L PDAC, adjuvant PDAC, and a major NSCLC business, all as though they were approved rather than in Phase 3. My base case grants a successful PDAC franchise *and* one NSCLC win and still lands at $108. To reach today's price you need ~$15–18B of peak sales — more than any published estimate, including RBC's bullish $11.5B.

This is a case where the sell-side and I are looking at the same facts and disagreeing about what is already in the price. The mean target of ~$223 is *at* the current price — the sell-side itself sees no upside from here, which is unusual for a Strong Buy consensus and tells you the rating reflects enthusiasm for the asset rather than a view on the multiple.

**The asymmetry is unattractive at this level.** Bull is +21%. Bear is −80% or worse. The binary events that resolve the gap — RASolve 301 in 2027, RASolute 303 — are one to two years away, and you are paying full franchise value to wait for them. Note also that both the approval and the ASCO plenary have now passed: the two largest de-risking catalysts are behind the stock, and the next meaningful one is a year out.

**On position sizing.** If you own it from lower levels, this is a hold — the franchise is real, the balance sheet is funded, and selling a first-in-class oncology platform outright because it got expensive is how you miss the compounders. Trim into strength above $230 to manage concentration. If you do not own it, wait. Biotech at all-time highs on approval day, priced above the most bullish analyst peak-sales estimate, is the single worst entry point in the cycle. A Phase 3 wobble, a slow first commercial quarter, or a broad biotech drawdown will offer a better one.

**What would change my mind, upward:** a clean RASolute 303 win in 1L PDAC, or RASolve 301 hitting in 2L NSCLC with an HR below 0.65. Either would move base-case peak sales toward $15B and fair value toward $180–200. Also watch Q3/Q4 revenue — if the launch materially beats RBC's $28M/$148M, the ramp assumption compresses and the base case rises.
